How much does a small business website cost in 2026?

Straight talk from a developer who builds these for a living, on what actually changes the price.

The short answer

There’s no single number, because “a website” covers wildly different jobs. A one-page site with your services and a contact button is a small project. A site with a lead inbox, an owner admin panel and bilingual content is bigger. A custom web app or platform is bigger again, usually scoped and priced in phases. The honest answer is: it depends on what you actually need, and any developer who gives you a number before asking what that is is guessing.

Freelancers and agencies charge differently for the same scope mostly because of overhead, not skill — an agency with a team and an office has more cost to cover than one developer does.

What actually drives the price

  1. Number of unique page types. Ten service pages built on one layout cost far less than ten different layouts.
  2. Who writes the words. Writing good copy (and translating it) is real work. If you bring finished text, the price drops.
  3. Things that store data. A form that emails you is cheap. Logins, bookings, menus you edit yourself and payments all need a database and an admin panel.
  4. Languages. A proper Spanish version (not machine-translated) adds time, and it opens a whole second market.
  5. Integrations. Google Business Profile, payment processors, scheduling tools, SMS.

Monthly costs nobody mentions

The build is a one-time cost. Watch the monthly ones:

  • Hosting: a simple static site can be hosted for $0–$5/month. Sites with a database usually run $7–$30/month.
  • Domain: about $12–$20/year.
  • Website builders (Wix, Squarespace and similar): $17–$50+/month, forever, and you never own the code.
  • “Maintenance plans” of $100+/month only make sense if they include real work every month.

Red flags when you get a quote

  • No written price before work starts.
  • You don’t end up owning your domain, or they won’t say who does.
  • A guaranteed #1 Google ranking.
  • A “free website” that comes with a long contract.
  • A desktop-only demo. Most of your customers will see your site on a phone.
